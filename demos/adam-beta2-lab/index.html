PANEL 1 / REDDI'S COUNTEREXAMPLE

Expected direction vs. stochastic direction

One variable · x ∈ [−1, 1]
At every step the gradient is g = +10 with probability 0.1, or g = −1 with probability 0.9.
Expected gradient E[g] = 0.1·10 − 0.9·1 = +0.1, so F(x) = 0.1·x and the minimizer is x* = −1.

Predict: SGD moves by −α·g. Adam moves by −α·m̂/√v̂. Starting from x = 0, where does each end up when β₂ = 0.5? When β₂ = 0.999?

Settings

Adam with bias correction, ε = 10⁻⁸, projected back onto [−1, 1] after every step. Expected steps are averaged over the second half of the run.

Iterate x_t

x* = −1 is the minimizer; x = +1 is the worst point

Which way does one step go, on average?

Arrows on the line x ∈ [−1, 1]. Faint: the two possible stochastic steps. Solid: the expected step. Top row: SGD, whose expected step is −E[g].

Why small β₂ goes the wrong way

When β₂ is small, v_t forgets quickly and v_t ≈ g_t², so the Adam step is ≈ −sign(g_t): signSGD. Every step has the same length, whatever the size of g.

E[−sign(g)] = 0.9·(+1) + 0.1·(−1) = +0.8  → moves right
−E[g] = −0.1  → moves left  (angle 180°)

The rare large gradient that should pull x left is shrunk to the same size as the frequent small gradients that push it right.

Why large β₂ fixes it

When β₂ → 1, v_t averages over many steps and v_t ≈ E[g²] = 0.1·100 + 0.9·1 = 10.9, almost a constant. The Adam step is then ≈ −g_t/√10.9, a rescaled SGD step, whose expectation −E[g]/√10.9 points to x* = −1.

Reddi et al. fixed β₂ first and then built a bad problem for it. In practice the problem is fixed and we tune β₂: a large enough β₂ (with β₁ < √β₂) converges. Same conclusion as the slide “Why is divergence not observed?”.

What is being trained? Read the experimental protocol.

Same data as Labs 1–2: 10,000 real MNIST digits at 14×14 (4000 train, 2000 test), standardized. ReLU MLPs with He initialization and softmax cross-entropy; “+ LN” adds LayerNorm after every ReLU. Adam: m ← β₁m + (1−β₁)g, v ← β₂v + (1−β₂)g², w ← w − α_t · (m/(1−β₁ᵗ)) / (√(v/(1−β₂ᵗ)) + 10⁻⁸). Schedules: constant α; reduce-on-plateau (÷10 after 10 checkpoints without improvement); warmup over 5% then cosine to 0. A run counts as diverged when the loss becomes non-finite.
