Same data and network family as Lab 1: 10,000 real MNIST digits at 14×14 (4000 train, 2000 test), a ReLU MLP with He initialization and softmax cross-entropy, plain SGD with mini-batches sampled with replacement from a seeded stream shared by all schedules. Schedules, with T iterations and peak α₀: constant α₀; constant α₀/10; step decay α₀ → α₀·f at 0.5T → α₀·f² at 0.75T; reduce-on-plateau (multiply by f after 10 checkpoints without a 0.01% improvement of the training loss, checkpoints every T/100 updates); 1/t: α₀/(1 + t/t₀) with t₀ = T/20; cosine α₀·½(1 + cos(πt/T)) to 0; warmup + cosine: linear warmup over the chosen fraction, then cosine to 0; WSD: warmup, constant α₀, linear decay to 0 over the last 20%.

A schedule is judged here by the training loss it reaches, which is what an optimizer controls. Test accuracy is shown too; it need not follow the same order. Try several seeds before concluding.

02 / WHY A SCHEDULE HELPS

Large α to move fast, small α to settle.

From Part I

E‖wt − w*‖² ≤ (1 − μα)2t · (initial error) + O(α σ²)

The first term shrinks faster when α is large; the second term — the floor — is smaller when α is small. No single constant α does both. A schedule starts large and ends small; the theory for diminishing stepsizes asks for Σ α_t = ∞ and Σ α_t² < ∞.

What to look for

Each drop of α gives a drop of the loss. Step decay and reduce-on-plateau produce a staircase: every time α is divided by 10, the floor moves down — exactly the O(α σ²) term.
Cosine spends longer at large α and still ends at α = 0. It makes fast progress early, then lowers α smoothly so that the floor keeps shrinking until the last update.
Speed of decay matters. 1/t is the textbook choice (Σ α_t = ∞, Σ α_t² < ∞) and does well here, but its result depends on t₀: decay too early and progress stalls, too late and the floor stays high. Cosine and WSD tie the shape to the total budget T instead.
